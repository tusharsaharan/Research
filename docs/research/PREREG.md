# Pre-registration — language-conditional certified routing (study R1)

Committed **before** any certification result on the `cert` split is looked
at. Changes after this commit are listed in the change log at the bottom, with
dates and reasons. Code: `backend/scripts/bench/` (`certify.py` is the analysis).

## 1. Question

When a cheap/premium LLM router is certified to lose quality on at most α of
queries, does that guarantee hold for Hinglish users separately, or only on
average? What does a per-language guarantee cost in savings?

## 2. Data

- Source items: GSM8K test (1,319 items, MIT licence), gold = number after `####`.
- Language variants per item: `english` (original) and `hinglish` (romanized
  Hindi-English, model-generated by gpt-oss-20b, then automatic checks:
  numbers, currency symbols, Roman script, detected as Hinglish and not
  identical to the English). Items failing checks are excluded from answering
  until repaired; every Hinglish item gets one human check (`verify_sheet.csv`),
  15% two checks for agreement. Exclusions are reported as counts, never silent.
- Splits by source item (seeded hash, `SEED = 20261015`): `scorer_train` 30%,
  `pilot` 10%, `cert` 60%. Both language versions of an item are always in the
  same split. `pilot` is used only for planning (label allocation); never for
  the reported results.
- Models (pair 1): cheap `openai/gpt-oss-20b`, premium `openai/gpt-oss-120b`
  on Groq, temperature 0, `reasoning_effort=low`, max 1,024 output tokens,
  system prompt fixed in `collect.py`. Pair 2 (another model family) is added
  later under the same protocol.
- Cost: provider-reported prompt and completion tokens (completion includes
  reasoning) × dated list prices in `backend/src/gateway/pricing.py`.

## 3. Definitions

- Group g ∈ {english, hinglish} (by construction here; in the live gateway by
  `language_group()`, whose accuracy is reported separately).
- u = 1 if the cheap answer is wrong and the premium answer is right (exact
  numeric match). Loss of routing x cheap at threshold λ: 1{s(x) ≤ λ}·u(x).
- Group risk R_g(λ): mean loss in group g = quality lost vs always-premium.
- Scorer s(x): logistic regression on multilingual MiniLM embeddings +
  language flag, code-mix ratio, log length; trained on `scorer_train` only.
- Threshold grid: 101 quantiles of scorer_train scores (fixed before calibration).

## 4. Methods compared

- **Marginal**: one threshold for all queries; fixed-sequence testing with
  Clopper–Pearson p-values P[Bin(n, α) ≤ k] at δ/|A|.
- **Per-language**: one threshold per group at δ/(K·|A|), K = 2.
- α grid A = {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}; δ = 0.10. Dividing by |A| makes any post-hoc
  choice of α from A valid.

## 5. Hypotheses (primary)

- **H1** Marginal certification violates α for the Hinglish group: violation
  rate (test risk > α across re-splits) > δ for at least one α ∈ A.
- **H2** Per-language certification keeps the violation rate ≤ δ (within
  binomial error) for both groups at every α ∈ A.
- **H3** Under a test-time language-mix shift (Hinglish share 10% → 90%), the
  marginal method's violation rate rises above δ while per-language stays ≤ δ.
- **H4** Per-language certification costs savings on Hinglish (lower cheap
  share than marginal); we report the size of this "certification tax".

## 6. Analysis

- 1,000 random calibration/test re-splits of `cert` (50/50 by item), seed 20261015.
- Report per method × α × group: violation rate with Clopper–Pearson 95% CI,
  mean risk, cheap share, real cost saving vs all-premium.
- Secondary: tokenizer tax (Hinglish/English prompt tokens on paired items),
  cheap and premium accuracy per group, automatic-check failure rate.
- A known-truth simulation must show violation ≤ δ for the certification code
  before real results are reported.
- No result is called "no loss" from a p-value alone; small-n results are
  labelled preliminary.

## 7. Stopping and reporting

Pair-1 analysis runs once all verified items are answered by both models.
Interim runs on partial data are allowed for debugging and are labelled
`preliminary` in every artefact; they do not change hypotheses or settings.
